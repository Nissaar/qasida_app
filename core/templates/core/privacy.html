Privacy

What this site knows about you

Written plainly, because a privacy notice nobody can read protects nobody. The short version: you can read the whole library without an account, and an account exists so that what you save follows you between devices.

Reading without an account

  • No account, no tracking cookie, and no advertising or analytics scripts of any kind.
  • The server writes an ordinary access log — the page asked for, the time, the address it was asked from — which is what any web server does and what makes it possible to tell a fault from an attack.
  • Whether you prefer the light or dark theme is kept in your own browser and never sent anywhere.
  • Pages you have opened are stored in your browser's cache so they can be read again without a signal. Clearing your browser data removes them.

If you open an account

An account holds a username, an email address, and a password that is stored only as a hash — nobody here can read it, including us. The email address is used to sign in and to reset the password, and to reply to you if you write in. It is never sold, shared or added to a mailing list.

Signed in, the site also keeps:

  • What you saved, with any note you wrote against it. The notes are yours; nothing shows them to anyone else.
  • What you have read recently, so you can find your way back to it. It is capped at a fixed number of entries, the oldest falling off, and you can clear the whole of it in one press from Recently read.
  • How you like verse laid out — text size, whether to show the transliteration and translation.
  • Corrections and contributions you sent, so you can see what became of them.

Editors see counts, never contents: how many works an account has saved, not which ones. There is no page in the administration of this site that lists one person's shelf or reading history.

Closing an account

Settings has a way out that needs your password and then deletes the account, what you saved, your reading history and your preferences. Two things survive it, both detached from your name: corrections that were already applied to a text, and texts you contributed that are now part of the library. Those have stopped being facts about you and become part of the record of where the library's contents came from.

Writing in

A message sent from the contact form is stored here and emailed to the library's address so it is not lost if mail fails. It carries whatever you put in it: your name, your email address, and what you wrote. A correction sent from a qasida page works the same way. Both are kept while they are useful and are used for nothing but answering you and fixing the text.

Other people's servers

  • Fonts and styling are fetched from Google's font service and a public code CDN, which means those services see that a browser at your address asked for a file. No page tells them which qasida you are reading.
  • Recordings are embedded from YouTube, and a page carrying one is a page YouTube knows you opened, under whatever terms your own YouTube session has. A work with no recording loads nothing from them.
  • Mail — password resets, replies, notifications — passes through an email provider, as all email does.
  • Scans and text are served from this site's own server. Nothing about your reading is sent to a third party.

Cookies

Two, both necessary and neither for tracking: one that keeps you signed in, and one that protects forms from being submitted by another site on your behalf. If you do not tick "keep me signed in", the first lasts only until you close the browser. There is nothing to consent to, which is why there is no banner asking you to.

Asking, correcting, or being forgotten

Write to {{ contact_email }} for a copy of what is held about you, to have something corrected, or to have it removed. Closing your account does most of it immediately, without having to ask anyone.
