What happens to it

  1. 1. It waits. Everything sent in goes into one queue, in the order it arrived.
  2. 2. Someone reads it. A text is checked against whatever source you gave before it becomes a record — the same reading every crawled work gets, for the same reason: a mistake published as scripture is worse than a gap.
  3. 3. You are told. Accepted or not, you get an email and it appears on your contributions page with a link to the finished work.

Before you send a text

  • Say where it comes from. A book and page, a recording, whom you had it from — this is what makes it checkable, and an unsourced text is much harder to accept.
  • Only send what is free to republish. A translation still under someone's copyright cannot go up, however good it is.
  • One verse to a line, with a blank line between stanzas. It is not fussiness: it is what lets the text be set beside its transliteration.
  • If the text only exists as a photograph of a page, send it to {{ contact_email }} instead — scans are welcome and are read here with OCR.
